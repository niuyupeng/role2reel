# Playable scenes and consequential dialogue

Dialogue is not alternating exposition. A person uses a partial judgment to change another person while protecting something of their own.

## Define the scene contract

Before drafting, establish:

- observable place, time, and physical constraints;
- objective facts already true;
- the scene-level dramatic question;
- each character's private goal, acceptable loss, and feared exposure;
- what each character knows, suspects, misunderstands, and believes the others know;
- which locked life-path nodes or shared-context keys the scene can activate;
- starting power, intimacy, obligation, and emotional residue;
- the intended or plausible ending state;
- any lines, facts, tone, rating, duration, or continuity that must be preserved.

When the supplied character path is explicitly a `light` behavior fixture, preserve that scope in the delivered artifact with a short non-dialogue label such as `评估范围：light 行为样例；不作为 deep 小传证据`. Do not make an actor say the label, and do not let a good short scene stand in for the unperformed deep-biography workflow.

Do not force a predetermined ending when the user asks for exploratory simulation. Otherwise use the ending state to prevent drift.

## Build a beat map

A beat begins when a character adopts a tactic and ends when new evidence or resistance changes what someone can do next. Track changes in at least one of:

- information;
- belief or certainty;
- intention or tactic;
- power or obligation;
- intimacy or trust;
- emotional control;
- physical position or possession.

Do not equate a beat with a sentence or every speaker change.

## Generate exchange by exchange

For high-stakes or knowledge-sensitive dialogue:

1. Identify the perceived cue.
2. Apply that character's knowledge boundary, locked life-path residue, relevant memory, and current relationship.
3. Form an appraisal, doubt, or question, then identify the first impulse before professional or social filtering.
4. Choose what the character wants the other person to do, reveal, believe, or feel.
5. Decide whether to respond, suppress, redirect, delay, or withhold; then select the smallest playable surface: action, spatial relation, gaze, expression, pause, silence, or dialogue. Dialogue may be empty.
6. Determine what the other person can actually observe.
7. Update the next turn's conditions.

This loop prevents characters from sharing the author's omniscience.

## Surface natural dialogue

Natural lines often use:

- implication through a concrete detail;
- literal answers that avoid the real question;
- questions whose social purpose differs from their grammar;
- partial agreement, qualification, correction, or withdrawal;
- asymmetry: one person pursues while another redirects;
- silence or practical action that becomes an answer;
- a shared look, code, joke, or taboo whose mutual knowledge is established in the relationship ledger;
- breath groups that an actor can say under the current emotion;
- relationship-specific address, politeness, aggression, or humor.

For Chinese dialogue, preserve meaningful subjects only when needed, allow context-dependent omission, and use particles or self-correction only when they carry attitude, hierarchy, hesitation, or rhythm. Do not spray colloquial filler over essay syntax.

## Require consequence, not constant information

An exchange passes when it changes the next move. It may:

- force an answer or create a debt;
- reveal how much someone knows;
- shift who controls the topic;
- increase or reduce distance;
- expose a vulnerability;
- close one tactic and open another;
- cause a physical action with downstream effect.

A line may repeat words while changing pressure. A line may add no fact while changing the relationship.

Do not require a line for every beat. Prefer a nonverbal surface when speech would only repeat the image, narrate private analysis, or explain information already mutually known. Shared memory may compress an exchange only to the depth established in the relationship ledger; a glance cannot transmit a new proposition.

Run a **mute pass** after the first draft. Remove each line and replay the beat with only the visible action and established context. Restore the line only when removing it changes what the listener can choose, know, refuse, or do next. A maximum of two lines means zero, one, or two—not an invitation to fill both slots. Delete a command when the partner is already doing the commanded action, and delete a statement of trust, solidarity, patience, or joint decision when distance, waiting, turn-taking, or division of the task already makes that relationship move legible. Do not replace a line with an invented prop ritual merely to keep the speaker active.

Coordination proves that a character decoded a shared cue; it does not by itself express that character's distinct private residue. When the shared-context contract says informed participants remember the event differently, give that difference one playable modulation beyond generic compliance—such as delay, altered distance, avoidance of the cue object, incomplete eye contact, or a different way of taking up the agreed task—without explaining the old event aloud.

Keep a shared-context test causally clean. Use the supplied dispute, object, and immediate task; do not invent an independent emergency, hazard, or command that would make an uninformed person perform the same state change. Otherwise the visible coordination no longer demonstrates that the established cue did any work.

Do not impose a universal short-line rule. A character may speak at length when the speech itself is the chosen action and the relationship, risk, breath, and tactic sustain it.

## Common failures and repairs

| Failure | Repair |
|---|---|
| Characters explain the visible present | Let them infer from details, pursue private aims, or resist the implied issue |
| Every secret leaks at the right dramatic moment | Reapply knowledge boundaries and second-order beliefs |
| Everyone speaks in complete balanced paragraphs | Restore unequal risk, status, breath, and tactical asymmetry |
| Personality is only tone or a catchphrase | Change noticed evidence, attribution, judgment, and decision threshold |
| Occupation or education becomes the personality | Run the locked life path and relationship first; let the role supply only demonstrated means or constraints |
| Backstory returns as autobiographical explanation | Keep the private causal model; surface only the present tactic, action, omission, or line |
| People explain a shared event to equally informed friends | Use the established shared-memory key, look, code, joke, avoidance, or silence unless the explanation itself is a tactic |
| Every beat contains dialogue | Remove lines that do no state work and allow gaze, expression, action, or silence to carry the beat |
| Dialogue repeats what the audience can see | Keep only a line that changes interpretation, power, intimacy, risk, obligation, certainty, or the next tactic |
| The line limit becomes a quota | Run the mute pass; zero lines is a complete result when action already changes the next choice |
| A protective move becomes a verbal command | Preserve the other person's agency; open space, wait, or ask only for information/action that is still missing |
| Every line advances plot facts | Allow exchanges that alter power, intimacy, obligation, or certainty |
| Subtext means arbitrary vagueness | Make the hidden objective precise and the surface tactic playable |
| Emotion appears before its cause | Restore cue, comprehension beat, first response, chosen mask, and residue |
| The scene ends with a slogan or summary | End on the new relationship, choice, image, or unresolved pressure actually earned |

## Performance pass

Read the scene aloud or simulate actor breath. Check that interruptions have targets, pauses have cognitive or emotional work, gestures are physically possible, and a performer can identify an action verb for each beat. Remove stage directions that dictate facial parts without a causal performance event.
