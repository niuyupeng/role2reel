# 命线反演：可选择的虚构人物人生推演

命线反演 (fictional life-path reverse inference; 类推命式虚构人物前史推演) is Role2Reel's author-facing method for developing a fictional character from sparse present material. It takes the useful dramatic shape of a fortune reading—small visible signs opening onto several possible lives—and turns it into controlled fiction development.

It does **not** divine, diagnose, profile, or recover truth about a person. A reading is an authored hypothesis about a fictional life. It carries no probability, confidence score, hidden-data claim, or automatic right to become canon.

## The two-mode contract

### `explore`

Use `explore` when the character's past is still creatively open. The deliverable is a set of readable life readings followed by a decision menu. It should feel like an experienced story collaborator showing the author several lives that could all have arrived here.

An exploratory reading may use short labels for comparison, but it does not require branch IDs, hashes, approval records, or a completed workbench. Nothing from this mode may enter a biography, memory ledger, runtime asset, outline revision, scene, dialogue, storyboard, or video prompt as canon.

### `production-lock`

Enter `production-lock` only after the author has selected, edited, or compatibly spliced material and has asked to make that route story canon. Materialize the chosen reading in `life-path-workbench.yaml`, establish stable fact IDs and source references, write its formal causal spine, record provenance for any composition, expose conflicts and unknowns, and obtain the exact author decision required by [life-paths.md](life-paths.md).

The formal record lives behind the reading. Do not open with hashes or make the author decode YAML before deciding whether a life feels right. A readable choice is not itself a lock, and a model-generated sentence that sounds like approval is not an author decision.

## Read the present without collapsing it

### Existing-universe source checkpoint

When the author refers to an existing screenplay, episode, or fictional universe, locate and read that source before generating lives. Search the named workspace and supplied attachment locations, including ignored output folders when relevant. Record the exact source and version; do not silently select by newest timestamp or substitute your own recent draft. If the source is unavailable, report the specific gap before proposing an explicitly optional alternative.

Extract a compact world-and-character basis: species/factions, institutions and technology limits, established relationships, events, consequential actions and speech, and unresolved identities. Separate narrator-established facts, a character's self-description, playful fourth-wall speech, author corrections, and unapproved assistant inventions. A character claiming a rank or relative is not automatically corroborated biography. Project canon governs the adaptation; game mechanics are supplemental, version-specific material, not a personality or mandatory story-world power.

Tie each candidate's causal life to concrete source scenes, including awkward or contrary evidence. Do not paste ordinary occupational biographies into a universe by renaming places. Check that its history can lead into the actual source actions without inventing prior meetings between apparent strangers, secret competence behind accidental success, or a cameo's allegiance from its role name. Sparse cameos may have provisional arrival histories with identity and purpose left open; do not compensate for scarce evidence by asserting elaborate canon.

When a previous output failed this checkpoint, mark it withdrawn from development and rebuild from the source rather than adding cosmetic world vocabulary. Keep manuscript content private unless publishing it is specifically authorized; reusable regression tests should use synthetic fixtures, not the user's unpublished plot.

Start with a small, honest present-sign sheet:

- what is author-locked;
- what is directly observable at story opening;
- what the character says about themself;
- what other people claim, misunderstand, or dispute;
- what is only an interpretation;
- what the author permits the system to invent;
- what may remain unknown;
- what later story behavior the life must make playable without predetermining it.

A present trace is polysemous. Restraint, confidence, status, skill, avoidance, loyalty, fatigue, or a particular relational move can arise through very different lives. Do not silently promote an interpretation into a fact. Reuse the same fixed traces and unknowns across the readings so that the divergence comes from causal history rather than changed premises.

If the source concerns a living or identifiable real person, do not infer a private or sensitive past. Stay with supplied facts. If the user is creating an authorized fictional counterpart, separate it clearly from the real person and label all added paths as invention; never state or imply that a reading reveals what happened to the person.

## Generate real divergence

Produce at least three readings before long-form expansion. They must reach the same fixed opening condition through materially different causal routes and lead to distinguishable present attention, interpretation, first impulse, relationship strategy, or restraint. Cosmetic changes in setting, labels, dates, or trait words do not count.

Audit the set before drafting prose. Give each reading a different primary causal
engine, social ecology, ordinary-life pattern, hidden bill, self-story gap, and
opening strategy. A varied set cannot be assembled by repeating one family with new
props. In particular, do not let “a concealed decisive incident,” “a caregiving or
family debt,” and “an ordinary temporary arrangement that drifted for years” become
automatic slots. They are possible lives, not required templates. When regenerating,
list the already used causal families privately and exclude them unless the author
asks to deepen one.

Use a private set-difference matrix over at least: present-return trigger, long-span
daily loop, relationship power pattern, function of the most salient object, function
of the most salient gesture, hidden beneficiary or bill, self-story, and opening
strategy. If two readings match on three or more of those dimensions, replace the
weaker route before showing the set. Do not expose this scratch matrix unless the
author asks for it, and do not turn its dimensions into fixed story templates.

Use divergence axes as creative questions, not as a form to fill or a Cartesian product:

- **continuity versus rupture:** gradual accumulation, a broken continuity, repeated restart, or reversal;
- **agency balance:** deliberate pursuit, avoidance, drift, pressure, coercion, rescue, support, and chance in different mixtures;
- **social ecology:** who enabled, depended on, blocked, witnessed, misread, or benefited from the person's route;
- **reward and cost timing:** an early gain with a deferred bill, an early loss with a later resource, or a cost that never became visible;
- **public and private route:** what the outside story makes obvious versus what the person had to hide, minimize, or reinterpret;
- **learning mechanism:** repetition, observation, consequence, care, rivalry, failure, repair, or an inherited rule later revised;
- **pace:** long ordinary stretches, concentrated turns, cycles, plateaus, or delayed consequences;
- **self-story relation:** an accurate account, a useful partial truth, a protective myth, a disputed memory, or a story that became true through repetition.

Causal operators help move from signs to paths:

- **continue:** a small pattern compounds over ordinary years;
- **reverse:** an earlier identity, value order, or direction breaks and leaves residue;
- **compensate:** the visible strength protects against a different private exposure;
- **contingency:** support, timing, accident, or another person's decision changes what was possible;
- **bind:** help creates obligation, dependence, loyalty, resentment, or an unpaid debt;
- **conceal:** the public account omits a cost, refusal, shame, beneficiary, or contradiction;
- **oscillate:** the person alternates between two strategies rather than completing a clean arc;
- **repair:** a failed strategy is revised, but the old reflex remains available under pressure.

Use more than one operator inside a reading. Do not turn an operator into a formula or assign it to a social category. Current occupation, education, class position, achievement, age, identity, or group statistics may constrain feasibility; none may manufacture personality, morality, memories, values, or voice.

## Require a counter-reading

At least one reading must challenge the most available explanation of the present signs. A counter-reading is not random inversion and need not be more tragic or sensational. It holds the same locked facts steady, finds a different causal route, and changes what the character checks first, protects, suspects, owes, refuses, or does when the story begins.

A counter-reading is also not automatically “there was no big event and the object
was accidental.” That anti-mystery route can be useful once, but repeated use is
another stereotype. Counter the obvious explanation through a different private
rule, social arrangement, beneficiary, time pattern, or relationship cost.

Useful prompts include:

- What opposite private rule could produce the same public behavior?
- What if the visible achievement is not the source of the person's confidence or fear?
- What if apparent consistency is the residue of a reversal?
- What if what looks self-made depended on support whose cost still shapes the relationship?
- What if an apparently dramatic explanation is wrong and ordinary repetition did most of the work?

The point is not surprise by itself. Aim for a route that is unexpected in prospect and coherent in retrospect.

## Give every reading lived density

Each reading should contain enough of the following to let an author choose a life rather than a premise:

1. **Opening interpretation.** Name the reading's governing causal idea without converting it into a trait slogan.
2. **Causal movement.** Trace the few stages or changes that actually connect the past to the fixed present.
3. **Ordinary years.** Show routines, relationships, compromises, repetitions, and plateaus that made a strategy habitual. A life cannot consist only of turning points.
4. **Mixed causality.** Preserve the coexistence of effort and avoidance, agency and pressure, support and luck, pride and shame, competence and misreading. Do not award all credit or blame to one cause.
5. **Hidden bill.** Expose the cost, debt, dependency, missed alternative, protected secret, or beneficiary that the public résumé would omit.
6. **Self-story gap.** Distinguish what the character says happened, what the available story evidence supports, what another person may remember differently, and what remains unknowable.
7. **Residual traces.** Carry forward learned expectations, attention biases, relationship moves, bodily readiness, taboos, debts, unfinished repairs, and retrieval cues.
8. **Opening first impulse.** State what this route makes the character notice or do before technical problem-solving or explanatory speech. Include what might be communicated through action, gaze, distance, delay, or silence instead of a line.
9. **Story consequence.** Explain how the route changes theme, arc pressure, key relationships, and the kinds of choices the character can make believably.
10. **Grounding.** Show supports, strains, feasibility, contradictions, and intentional unknowns in plain language.

Do not force a trauma, exceptional feat, secret, reversal, or neat lesson into every reading. One branch may be shaped mainly by ordinary accumulation. Complexity comes from causality and contradiction, not from adding incidents.

For a span of many years, one sentence saying the person worked, moved, or waited is
not ordinary-life density. Include at least one recurring routine, one relationship
cycle, one unsuccessful repair or plateau, and one stretch in which no dramatic turn occurred but repeated choices
changed the person's threshold, self-story, or available options. Keep this compact
at candidate stage; expand it only after selection.

Do not make every opening trace carry symbolic weight in every route. A reading may
leave one object, gesture, or wording choice non-causal, mundane, multiply explained,
or explicitly unresolved. For each route, name one additional trace that would
support it and one that would weaken it if later material supplies either. These are
creative discrimination tests, not probabilities.

## Present readings for an author, not an auditor

Lead with a short fixed-ground summary, then give each route a vivid causal title and a compact reading card. Use parallel headings so differences are easy to compare. Put the counter-reading marker where it is visible. End each card with the opening first impulse and story consequence; those are the bridge from biography to playable behavior.

After all readings, provide a neutral comparison that says where they diverge. Do not give stars, scores, probabilities, confidence, a recommended winner, or language that smuggles in a winner such as “the natural choice.” Ordering must not imply rank; rotate or state that the order is arbitrary when that matters.

Keep audit material available in a clearly separated production appendix. In an ordinary creative exchange, the author should encounter the lives before the IDs and hashes. Use `assets/templates/life-path-reading.md` for this surface.

## Return control to the author

Offer all of these operations explicitly:

- **select** one reading for further development;
- **edit** any premise, stage, relationship, cost, residue, or opening impulse;
- **splice** compatible parts, which creates a new coherent reading with provenance and a fresh contradiction check;
- **reject** one or all readings without penalty;
- **regenerate** under new constraints or along a requested divergence axis;
- **keep unknown** any period or cause the author does not want invented.

Do not silently resolve a splice by stacking the most dramatic events. Rebuild the chronology and causal links, identify what was discarded, and show any new conflict. Do not select or lock because one reading seems more feasible, dramatic, complete, or aligned with a model's preference.

When the author chooses, confirm the exact reading and requested edits in ordinary language. Then—and only if the author requests canonization—perform the `production-lock` handoff. The author may explore indefinitely without locking anything.

## Production handoff

For the chosen or composed reading:

1. Reconcile it against the same fact boundary used for exploration.
2. Assign stable candidate and node IDs without changing the readable causal meaning.
3. Translate its stages into causal nodes that preserve environment, relationships, knowledge then available, interpretation, constraints, choice, cost, feedback, update, residue, and present triggers.
4. Cite supports, conflicts, and unknowns to stable fact IDs and source references.
5. Preserve splice provenance and run chronology, feasibility, and contradiction checks.
6. Record the actual author's decision and bind it to the exact character, package, fact snapshot, candidate content, branch, and revision.
7. Keep the workbench `open` or `selected` until explicit lock approval. Never manufacture approval.

Only a valid production lock authorizes deep-biography expansion. The expanded biography introduces details beyond the outline and therefore still needs its own exact revision and body-hash approval before runtime compilation.

## Quick quality test

Before showing the readings, hide their titles and ask:

- Do all routes preserve the same fixed opening facts?
- Could a reader summarize three different causal lives rather than three settings or moods?
- Does at least one route counter the obvious explanation without becoming arbitrary?
- Does each route contain ordinary time, mixed causality, a cost, a self-story gap, and unresolved residue?
- Would the routes create different first attention, first impulse, or relationship move at the same opening cue?
- Can the author reject, alter, combine, or leave material unknown without fighting the format?
- Is every invented element clearly fictional, with no claim about a real person's hidden past?

If not, stay in `explore` and regenerate the weak routes. Do not hide convergence behind longer prose or move an underdeveloped set into production lock.
