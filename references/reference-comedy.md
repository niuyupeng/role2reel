# Reference-led comedy adaptation

Use only when the author requests reference borrowing or comedy. Keep a current project brief containing genre/tone, cast, identities versus abilities, length, named film/version, number of reference moments, and exclusions. Later author corrections supersede earlier proposals; archive the prior brief instead of letting different drafts silently disagree.

For each selected reference, record the source URL/local asset, version, actually observed interval or sampled frames, original dramatic/comedic mechanism, permitted properties to borrow, and the target beat. Distinguish search descriptions, plot summaries, sampled frames, actually reviewed clips, and a complete film viewing. Downloads and source-page transcripts can cover different intervals. Do not claim precise full-film timestamps from a clip-relative clock, or a blooper is the released scene.

If all references must be funny, each must have an identifiable comic setup and payoff; visual fame alone is insufficient. A heroic sacrifice cannot meet that constraint merely by adding a joke afterward. Candidate mechanisms include status reversal, confident plans undermined by specifics, literal interpretation, wasted effort, incongruous behavior under pressure and delayed prop payoff. Choose mechanisms appropriate to the author's characters; do not mechanically require all of them or rely on meme density.

Build one causal event from the references. For each gag, show setup, expectation, character choice, reversal and consequence; let one prop or unresolved claim return when useful. Preserve enough time for recognition and reaction. Do not add explanatory punchlines after a legible visual payoff.

Each new character must have a stake, a choice, an effect on another person's next move and an aftermath, not just an assigned gag. A title such as scapegoat is not permission to invent game powers or make the person passive. If the author has not supplied an important rule, ask narrowly or keep the behavior explicitly provisional.

Before delivery, check character/knowledge integrity, prop ownership, location, timing budget, protected jokes, whether the reader can understand without the reference, and whether the reference remains recognizable in the authorized way. A model's opinion that something is funny is not audience acceptance. Preserve source material as reference; downloads do not grant reuse/commercial rights.
